# Budget — Narrative Outline

## Headline Thesis

India's government spends ₹50 lakh crore a year, and for every rupee it spends, it has to borrow a quarter of it — the budget is an act of faith that today's debt will pay for tomorrow's growth.

## Arc Type

**Follow-the-money** — trace a single rupee from the citizen's pocket through the government's hands to the outcome on the other side.

## Opening Hook

"The government spends ₹137 on you every single day. Where does it get that money — and where does it go?"

Start with the per-capita daily spend number. It's concrete, personal, and immediately raises the question: where does that ₹137 come from? This inverts the current structure (which saves per-capita for last) and puts the reader at the center from beat one.

## Beats (ordered)

### Beat 1 — "Your daily share"
- **Narrative function**: Anchor the entire budget in something personal. The government spends ₹137/day on you. That's more than the daily wage of a rural labourer. This is YOUR money story.
- **Key data**: Per-capita daily expenditure (₹137). Breakdown: ₹X on debt interest, ₹Y on defence, ₹Z on transfers to your state.
- **Emotional register**: curiosity
- **Transition**: "But where does this money come from? Let's trace the rupee backward."

### Beat 2 — "Where the rupee comes from"
- **Narrative function**: Argue that India's revenue depends on a narrow tax base. Of every rupee spent, about 75 paise comes from actual revenue — a mix of income tax, GST, corporate tax, and non-tax sources. The rest is borrowed.
- **Key data**: Waffle chart. Total receipts (₹ lakh crore). Income tax vs GST vs corporate tax shares. The key number: "X paise of every rupee spent comes from taxes."
- **Emotional register**: curiosity shifting to tension
- **Transition**: "75 paise earned. But the government spends a full rupee. Where do the remaining 25 paise come from?"

### Beat 3 — "The borrowing gap"
- **Narrative function**: Argue that the deficit is not irresponsible — it's structural. India borrows because revenue hasn't caught up with ambition. The fiscal deficit at X% of GDP means the government is betting on future growth to repay present borrowing.
- **Key data**: Deficit paise per rupee spent (hero number). Fiscal deficit as % of GDP. The rupee bar (earned vs borrowed).
- **Emotional register**: tension
- **Transition**: "This borrowing pattern isn't new. Zoom out two decades and you see the gap has been a feature, not a bug — except when it explodes."

### Beat 4 — "Two decades of the gap"
- **Narrative function**: Argue that the deficit is cyclical and crisis-driven. The 2020-21 COVID spike to 9.2% of GDP was a deliberate choice — spend to survive. The trajectory since has been disciplined contraction. But the structural gap between expenditure and receipts has never closed.
- **Key data**: Line chart — 20-year expenditure vs receipts trend. Fiscal deficit % GDP with COVID spike callout. Growth multiple (expenditure Xed over the period).
- **Emotional register**: surprise (the COVID spike), then cautious resolution
- **Transition**: "So the government plans to borrow. But does it stick to the plan? Ministries are notorious for over- or under-spending."

### Beat 5 — "Plans vs reality"
- **Narrative function**: Argue that budgeting is an accountability mechanism, and execution gaps reveal priorities. Some ministries consistently overshoot (crisis response, interest payments), others consistently undershoot (rural development, health). The gap between Budget Estimate and Actual is where political will meets bureaucratic capacity.
- **Key data**: Deviation chart — overspenders vs underspenders by ministry. Biggest overspend name + %. Biggest underspend name + %.
- **Emotional register**: surprise (who overspends?) shifting to mild anger (who underspends?)
- **Transition**: "Now we know where it comes from and whether plans hold. Let's follow the money out — where does every crore actually land?"

### Beat 6 — "Where the money goes"
- **Narrative function**: Argue that the expenditure treemap reveals embedded priorities. Interest payments and defence are the two largest non-negotiable blocks. What remains — the discretionary budget for development — is smaller than people think.
- **Key data**: Treemap of ministry-wise expenditure. Interest payments as share of total. The "discretionary" remainder after non-negotiables.
- **Emotional register**: tension (how little is discretionary)
- **Transition**: "The treemap shows categories. But money doesn't sit in categories — it flows. Let's see the full river from source to destination."

### Beat 7 — "Follow the flow"
- **Narrative function**: The Sankey diagram is the capstone visualization — the single image that connects every beat so far. Revenue sources on the left, expenditure on the right, the government in the middle. The width of each stream is the argument.
- **Key data**: Sankey: tax categories flowing through to expenditure destinations. The thickest stream. The thinnest.
- **Emotional register**: resolution (the full picture clicks)
- **Transition**: "Finally, this money doesn't stay in Delhi. Nearly one in four rupees flows out to states."

### Beat 8 — "Where it lands"
- **Narrative function**: Argue that the budget is spatially uneven. Per-capita transfers vary wildly across states — a citizen in a northeastern state receives multiples of what a citizen in a large southern state receives. This isn't inequity — it's equalization — but the map makes the variation visceral.
- **Key data**: Choropleth map of per-capita transfers to states. Highest vs lowest state comparison.
- **Emotional register**: surprise (the variation), then understanding (equalization logic)
- **Transition**: None needed — this is the landing.

## Internal Consistency Rules

- **Single denominator throughout**: "per rupee spent." Not "per rupee earned." The rupee spent is the total budget, and borrowings are part of how it's funded. This keeps all paise-framing consistent.
  - Beat 2: "X paise of every rupee spent comes from taxes"
  - Beat 3: "Y paise of every rupee spent is borrowed"
  - These two must add to 100.
- **Time period default**: FY 2025-26 (Budget Estimate) for all single-year references. Trend sections explicitly label the range (e.g., "2004-05 to 2025-26").
- **₹137/day**: Computed from total expenditure / population / 365. Must match the per-capita data used in Beat 1 and the KeyTakeaways pills.
- **Fiscal deficit % GDP**: Same number in Beat 3 (deficit section) and Beat 4 (trends section, latest year). Source: Union Budget documents.

## Closing

The reader walks away understanding one thing: this is YOUR money. ₹137 a day, from your pocket through a system that borrows a quarter of what it spends, channeled into debt servicing, defence, and transfers that reach your state unevenly. The budget isn't an abstract document — it's the single largest financial decision that affects every Indian, every day.

## Cross-Domain Connections

- **Beat 2 (Revenue) -> Economy**: Tax-to-GDP ratio connects to the Economy domain's fiscal section. India collects less tax relative to GDP than peer economies.
- **Beat 3 (Deficit) -> RBI**: The deficit is funded by government borrowing, which competes with private borrowing for bank funds — connecting directly to RBI's credit and liquidity sections.
- **Beat 4 (Trends) -> Economy**: The COVID deficit spike mirrors the Economy domain's -5.8% GDP contraction. Same crisis, different lens.
- **Beat 8 (Map) -> States**: Per-capita transfers connect directly to the States domain's fiscal federalism and regional inequality narratives.
