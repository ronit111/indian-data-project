# Census & Demographics -- Narrative Outline

## Headline Thesis
India has the world's largest young population, but the window to convert that into prosperity is closing fast -- and it's already shut in some states.

## Arc Type
Window-of-opportunity (asset exists but window is closing)

## Opening Hook
"For every 100 Indians, 68 are of working age. That ratio peaks around 2035 -- then it starts falling, permanently. What India does in the next decade decides the next century."

The hook is a countdown frame. Not "India is big" (everyone knows that). Instead: India's demographic structure is a ticking asset with an expiry date.

## Beats (ordered)

### Beat 1: The Scale
- **Beat name**: 1.45 billion and slowing
- **Narrative function**: Reframes the story away from "population explosion" toward deceleration. India isn't growing fast anymore. The growth rate has halved in two decades. The real story is the SHAPE of the population, not the size.
- **Key data**: Population line (2000-2024), growth rate falling from 1.9% to ~0.9%. India overtook China in 2023.
- **Emotional register**: Curiosity (challenging the "too many people" assumption)
- **Transition to next**: "The growth is slowing. But who are these 1.45 billion people? That's where the story gets interesting."

### Beat 2: The Dividend Window
- **Beat name**: A young nation's window
- **Narrative function**: ARGUES that India's age structure is a once-in-history asset. 68 of every 100 Indians are working-age. The dependency ratio is at its lowest point ever. But this isn't permanent -- the working-age share peaks around 2035 and then the elderly share starts climbing. The window is roughly 2020-2040.
- **Key data**: Age waffle (100-cell grid: young/working/elderly split), age composition line chart showing the crossover trends. The waffle is the hero viz -- visceral, immediate.
- **Emotional register**: Urgency (this is a countdown, not a celebration)
- **Transition to next**: "This dividend only pays off if the population is healthy and educated. Start with the basics: are families getting smaller? Are people living longer?"

### Beat 3: The Demographic Transition
- **Beat name**: Fewer babies, longer lives
- **Narrative function**: ARGUES that India has already completed most of its demographic transition -- fertility below replacement (2.1), life expectancy past 72 years. This is the mechanism behind the closing window: fewer children entering the pipeline means eventually fewer workers.
- **Key data**: Birth rate vs death rate (converging lines), fertility rate crossing below 2.1 (with reference line), life expectancy by gender (female edge visible).
- **Emotional register**: Surprise (most readers think India still has high birth rates)
- **Transition to next**: "But national averages mask a split so dramatic it's hard to believe these are the same country."

### Beat 4: Two Countries in One
- **Beat name**: Every child counts
- **Narrative function**: ARGUES that India's demographic story is really 36 different stories. A child born in Kerala has survival odds matching Western Europe. A child in some northern states faces odds comparable to sub-Saharan Africa. The state-level IMR choropleth is the gut-punch moment -- the gradient from green (Kerala, Goa) to red (MP, UP, Assam) makes the inequality visible.
- **Key data**: IMR line (national, 66 to under 25 -- dramatic progress), state-level IMR choropleth (the variation map). The choropleth is the hero viz of this beat.
- **Emotional register**: Anger/tension (this inequality is not natural, it's a policy choice)
- **Transition to next**: "Survival is step one. The next question: can these children read?"

### Beat 5: The Literacy Divide
- **Beat name**: The literacy revolution
- **Narrative function**: ARGUES that literacy is the gateway to every other opportunity -- and India has made enormous progress (from ~65% to ~74% in one decade), but the gender gap and state gap remain canyons. Kerala at 94% vs Bihar at 62%. The gender gap narrowed from 26 points to 14, but in Rajasthan it's still 20+.
- **Key data**: Literacy trend line (total, male, female), gender gap butterfly chart (15 states, sorted by gap), state choropleth.
- **Emotional register**: Hope mixed with frustration (progress is real but unevenly distributed)
- **Transition to next**: "And where these literate, healthy, working-age Indians live matters enormously for the economy."

### Beat 6: The Urbanization Gap
- **Beat name**: Urban India rising
- **Narrative function**: ARGUES that India is under-urbanized for its income level (35% vs global 56%). Rural India is still home to 2 in 3 Indians. Urbanization is happening, but slowly -- and concentrated in a dozen mega-cities while thousands of small towns stagnate. This has direct implications for the dividend: urban areas create most formal-sector jobs.
- **Key data**: Urban population % trend line, state choropleth of urbanization rates.
- **Emotional register**: Resolution/anticipation (sets up the question: can the dividend be captured?)
- **Transition to next**: (Closing section -- no transition needed)

## Internal Consistency Rules
- **Denominator**: Always "per 100 Indians" or "per 1,000" for rates. Never mix per-100 and per-1,000 in the same beat.
- **Time period**: Default is 2000-2024 for national trends. State data anchored to Census 2011 or latest available (SRS 2022 for IMR, NFHS-5 for immunization). Always label which year for state data.
- **Population figure**: Use "1.45 billion" throughout (not 145 crore, not 1.4 billion). This is the one number where we use the international format because the hero number is displayed as "1.45B."
- **Cross-beat numbers**: Growth rate (0.9%), fertility (below 2.1), literacy (74%), urbanization (35%), IMR (under 25 per 1,000) -- if any of these appear in multiple beats, they must use the same source and value.
- **State comparisons**: Always use the same pair (Kerala vs Bihar or equivalent) as the anchor contrast. Don't compare Kerala to MP in one beat and then to UP in another unless making a distinct point.

## Closing
The reader walks away with a reframing: India's demographic story is not about having too many people. It's about having the right people at the right time -- and the clock is running. The states that invested in health and education (Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Goa) are already reaping the dividend. The states that didn't (Bihar, UP, MP) have a decade to catch up before their window closes too. The question is no longer "can India feed 1.45 billion?" It's "can India employ, educate, and heal 1.45 billion before the window shuts?"
