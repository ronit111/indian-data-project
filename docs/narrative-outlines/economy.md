# Economy — Narrative Outline

## Headline Thesis

India's economy grows at 7% a year, but the average Indian earns ₹2 lakh — growth is real, but it hasn't reached most pockets yet. The gap between the macro headline and the micro reality is the central tension of Indian economics.

## Arc Type

**Contradiction** — India is simultaneously the world's fastest-growing major economy AND a country where the median citizen's purchasing power is eroding. Both things are true. The story is about why.

## Opening Hook

"India's GDP crossed ₹300 lakh crore. Your salary didn't notice."

The hero section should juxtapose two numbers: the GDP headline (impressive) and per-capita income (modest). The tension between them is the entire story. This replaces the current approach of leading with GDP growth rate alone, which reads like a report card rather than a felt experience.

## Beats (ordered)

### Beat 1 — "The headline vs your headline"
- **Narrative function**: Argue that GDP growth is real but unevenly distributed. 7% growth means the economy produced 7% more goods and services. But per-capita income — what the average Indian actually earns — grows slower because population grows too, and because the gains concentrate in specific sectors and geographies.
- **Key data**: GDP growth line (decade view) with pandemic valley. Per-capita GDP in rupees. The ratio: GDP grew X-fold in a decade, per-capita income grew Y-fold. The gap between X and Y is the story.
- **Emotional register**: curiosity turning to tension
- **Transition**: "If growth is real, why doesn't it feel real? Because prices eat into it."

### Beat 2 — "The price you pay for growth"
- **Narrative function**: Argue that inflation is the invisible tax on growth. When GDP grows 7% and inflation runs at 5%, your real gain is only 2%. Food inflation — the most regressive form — hits the poorest hardest because they spend 45% of income on food vs 25% for the middle class. The RBI's target band (2-6%) is not a technicality — it's a promise that growth will translate into real purchasing power.
- **Key data**: CPI headline inflation line with the 2-6% target band. Food CPI vs core CPI (the divergence is the argument). The "real growth" gap: nominal GDP growth minus inflation.
- **Emotional register**: tension (prices eroding gains)
- **Transition**: "Inflation is one leak. The government's own borrowing is another — when it borrows heavily, it competes with businesses for bank funds, pushing up interest rates for everyone."

### Beat 3 — "The government's IOUs"
- **Narrative function**: Argue that fiscal consolidation is not austerity — it's creating room for private investment. The fiscal deficit (spending minus revenue) peaked at 9.2% of GDP during COVID. The path back to 3% (the FRBM target) is a deliberate trade-off: less government borrowing today means cheaper loans for businesses and homebuyers tomorrow.
- **Key data**: Fiscal deficit % GDP bar chart with FRBM 3% target line. Revenue deficit overlay. The COVID spike as an annotated moment.
- **Emotional register**: surprise (COVID spike) then cautious hope (consolidation trajectory)
- **Transition**: "The domestic fiscal picture is one half. India's position in the global economy — what it buys, what it sells, what it saves in foreign currency — is the other half."

### Beat 4 — "India and the world"
- **Narrative function**: Argue that India's trade deficit is structural, not cyclical. We import oil, electronics, and gold; we export services (IT) and some manufacturing. The Current Account Deficit is the gap, and remittances from Indians abroad are the single biggest cushion. Forex reserves are the war chest — they exist so that a global oil shock doesn't cause a currency crisis that makes your petrol and phone prices spike overnight.
- **Key data**: Area chart (exports vs imports as % GDP). CAD overlay line. Forex reserves callout. Import cover months.
- **Emotional register**: tension (persistent deficit) then reassurance (reserves as shield)
- **Transition**: "So India imports more than it exports. But what does India actually produce? The composition of the economy tells you where the jobs and growth are coming from."

### Beat 5 — "What India makes, sells, and grows"
- **Narrative function**: Argue that India's economic structure is unusual — services dominate at ~50% of GDP (unlike China or Korea where manufacturing led). This is both strength (IT services, financial services) and vulnerability (services employ fewer people per unit of GDP than manufacturing). Construction and manufacturing are gaining ground, but the structural shift hasn't happened yet.
- **Key data**: Sector scorecard — current growth vs 5-year average, with GVA share annotations. Services vs industry vs agriculture shares.
- **Emotional register**: surprise (services dominance) then urgency (manufacturing gap)
- **Transition**: "The numbers say growth is real. The structure says it's concentrated. What does the road ahead look like?"

### Beat 6 — "The road ahead"
- **Narrative function**: Synthesize the contradiction. Growth is projected at 6.3-6.8% — still fast by global standards. But the question isn't whether India grows. It's whether the growth reaches the 60% of Indians who live in rural areas, the 45% still in agriculture, and the young people entering the workforce at 1.2 crore per year. The macro is promising. The micro is the test.
- **Key data**: Projection range card. CPI inflation projection. Fiscal deficit target. Current account. These are presented as a scorecard — not a celebration, but a challenge.
- **Emotional register**: resolution (clear-eyed, neither pessimistic nor cheerleading)
- **Transition**: None — this is the landing.

## Internal Consistency Rules

- **Dual framing**: Every macro number gets a micro translation. GDP in lakh crore AND per-capita. Growth rate AND what it means in real (inflation-adjusted) terms. Trade deficit AND what it means for your petrol price.
- **Time period default**: Latest available fiscal year (2025-26 projections where applicable, 2024-25 actuals otherwise). Trend charts cover a decade minimum.
- **Growth rate source**: All GDP growth figures from the same World Bank / MOSPI source. Real GDP (constant prices), not nominal.
- **Inflation-adjusted language**: When saying "real growth," always clarify (adjusted for inflation). The audience may not know the term.
- **No cheerleading, no catastrophizing**: The tone is "here's what the numbers say, and here's what they don't." India IS growing fast. The growth IS unevenly felt. Both are true.

## Closing

The reader walks away with a more nuanced take than "India's economy is booming." Yes, GDP is growing. But inflation erodes the gain, the fiscal deficit means the government is borrowing a quarter of what it spends, the trade deficit means we depend on the world for oil and electronics, and the growth is concentrated in services that don't employ as many people as manufacturing would. The economy is a glass that is genuinely half full — but which half you see depends on where you sit.

## Cross-Domain Connections

- **Beat 2 (Inflation) -> RBI**: Inflation targeting is the RBI's primary mandate. The Economy domain shows the outcome; the RBI domain shows the mechanism (repo rate, CRR, monetary policy decisions).
- **Beat 3 (Fiscal) -> Budget**: The fiscal deficit IS the budget's borrowing gap. Same number, different lens — the Economy domain contextualizes it as % of GDP; the Budget domain traces where the borrowed money goes.
- **Beat 4 (External) -> RBI**: Forex reserves are managed by the RBI. The Economy domain shows WHY reserves matter (trade deficit); the RBI domain shows HOW they're built and deployed.
- **Beat 5 (Sectors) -> Employment**: The sectoral composition directly determines employment patterns. Services produce GDP but fewer jobs per crore; manufacturing and construction are more labor-intensive.
- **Beat 5 (Sectors) -> States**: The service economy is concentrated in a few states (Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana). The agricultural economy is everywhere else. This connects to regional inequality.
