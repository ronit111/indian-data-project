# Education -- Narrative Outline

## Headline Thesis
India got 248 million children into classrooms -- and then discovered that showing up is not the same as learning.

## Arc Type
Cause-chain (triumph leads to complacency leads to crisis leads to root cause)

## Opening Hook
"India runs the largest school system on Earth. 248 million students. Primary enrollment above 100%. By any access metric, it's a success story. But ask a different question -- can a third-grader read a second-grade textbook? -- and the story collapses."

The hook sets up the central bait-and-switch: the enrollment numbers are a celebration, but the learning numbers are a crisis. The tension between these two facts drives the entire arc.

## Beats (ordered)

### Beat 1: The Enrollment Triumph
- **Beat name**: 248 million students
- **Narrative function**: ARGUES that India solved the access problem. Primary GER above 100%. Secondary GER nearly tripled in two decades. Tertiary enrollment climbing. This is a genuine civilizational achievement -- getting nearly every child into a classroom in a country this large and this poor. Let the reader feel the triumph first.
- **Key data**: GER by level (primary, secondary, tertiary) over time. Primary consistently above 100%, secondary rising from ~40% to ~80%, tertiary from ~10% to ~30%.
- **Emotional register**: Pride / celebration
- **Transition to next**: "But enrollment is not learning. Getting children into classrooms was the easy part. What happens inside them tells a different story."

### Beat 2: The Gender Convergence
- **Beat name**: Girls catch up
- **Narrative function**: ARGUES that the gender gap in enrollment has largely closed at the secondary level -- girls have nearly matched boys, and in some states surpassed them. This is a quiet revolution. But it sets up a darker question: if girls are enrolling, why do they still drop out more?
- **Key data**: Secondary GER by gender (male vs female lines converging over 2000-2022).
- **Emotional register**: Hope (a genuine bright spot)
- **Transition to next**: "Girls now match boys in enrollment. But the funnel narrows sharply after primary school."

### Beat 3: The Dropout Cliff
- **Beat name**: The dropout cliff
- **Narrative function**: ARGUES that India's pipeline leaks badly between primary and secondary. The average dropout rate roughly doubles from primary to secondary. State variation is enormous -- some states lose 1 in 3 students before they finish secondary school. This is where the enrollment triumph starts to crack.
- **Key data**: Primary vs secondary average dropout bars (national), state-level secondary dropout bar chart (top 15 worst states).
- **Emotional register**: Tension (the good news from Beat 1 starts unraveling)
- **Transition to next**: "But the students who stay -- are they actually learning?"

### Beat 4: The Quality Gap
- **Beat name**: Enrollment is not learning
- **Narrative function**: This is the emotional center of the arc. ARGUES that India's education crisis is not about access but about outcomes. ASER 2024 data: roughly 1 in 5 children in Class 3 can read a Class 2 textbook. Children are in school, but many cannot read or do basic math at their grade level. The choropleth makes the state variation visceral -- some states below 15%, others above 40%.
- **Key data**: State choropleth of % children in Std III who can read Std II text (ASER 2024). This is the hero visualization of the entire domain.
- **Emotional register**: Shock / anger (the system is failing the children who showed up)
- **Transition to next**: "The teacher at the front of the classroom matters more than any policy document."

### Beat 5: The Teacher Challenge
- **Beat name**: 1 teacher, 35 students
- **Narrative function**: ARGUES that teacher availability is a root cause of the quality gap. The national average PTR (25:1) masks extreme variation -- from 8 in Sikkim to 35 in Jharkhand. More teachers doesn't automatically mean better learning, but fewer teachers guarantees worse outcomes. The trend shows improvement, but not fast enough.
- **Key data**: PTR over time (primary and secondary lines), state-level PTR bar chart (top 15 worst).
- **Emotional register**: Frustration (structural problem, slow to fix)
- **Transition to next**: "Teachers need support. Schools need infrastructure. Both need money."

### Beat 6: The Spending Gap
- **Beat name**: 4% of GDP
- **Narrative function**: ARGUES that India systematically underspends on education relative to its ambitions. 4.1% of GDP vs the NEP 2020 target of 6%. The gap is not just a number -- it translates directly into larger class sizes, fewer trained teachers, and crumbling infrastructure. The reference line at 6% on the chart makes the shortfall visible at a glance.
- **Key data**: Education expenditure as % GDP (with 6% NEP reference line), education as % government spending.
- **Emotional register**: Urgency (the cause of the cause -- underfunding drives everything upstream)
- **Transition to next**: (Closing section -- no transition needed)

## Internal Consistency Rules
- **Denominator**: Always "% of enrolled students" for dropout rates, "% of age group" for GER, "% of GDP" for spending. Never mix denominators.
- **Time period**: 2000-2022 for national time series (World Bank). State data: UDISE+ 2022-23 for dropout/PTR, ASER 2024 for learning outcomes. Always label which survey for state data.
- **Student count**: "248 million" or "24.8 crore" -- use one format consistently. The hero uses "248M" (matching the "M" format in the hero display).
- **PTR framing**: Always "students per teacher" (not teacher-student ratio). Use "25 students per teacher" not "PTR of 25."
- **GER caveat**: Mention at first appearance that GER can exceed 100% due to over-age/under-age students. Don't repeat this caveat in later beats.
- **ASER framing**: "1 in 5 children in Class 3 can read a Class 2 textbook" -- this specific framing should be used consistently (not "20% literacy" or other paraphrases).

## Closing
The reader walks away understanding that India's education story has two chapters. Chapter one -- getting children into school -- is largely written, and it's a success. Chapter two -- making sure they actually learn -- is still being written, and the early results are alarming. The cause chain is clear: underspending leads to too few teachers, which leads to poor learning outcomes, which leads to dropout. The answer is not more enrollment drives. It's more investment in what happens after a child walks through the school door.

## Cross-Domain Connections
- **Beat 1 (Enrollment)** links to **Census** (the 248 million are the young cohort from the demographic dividend)
- **Beat 3 (Dropout Cliff)** links to **Employment** (dropout teens enter the informal workforce -- connecting education failure to employment informality)
- **Beat 4 (Quality Gap)** links to **Census / Literacy** (ASER reading levels connect to adult literacy outcomes a generation later)
- **Beat 6 (Spending Gap)** links to **Budget** (education's share of Union Budget expenditure) and **Healthcare** (both are chronically underfunded social sectors)
