# Elections -- Narrative Outline

## Headline Thesis

India's democracy is the world's largest experiment in participation. The data shows it works at the ballot box -- and struggles everywhere else.

## Arc Type

**Institutional-quality** -- who shows up (participation), who wins (power concentration), who represents you (candidate quality), and who is missing (gender). The arc celebrates the first, then interrogates the other three with escalating discomfort.

## Opening Hook

96.88 crore eligible voters. More people than Europe's entire population. In 2024, nearly two-thirds of them walked to a polling booth, waited in line, and pressed a button. This is the most staggering act of collective civic faith on the planet. But faith in what?

## Beats (ordered)

### Beat 1: The World's Largest Election (Turnout)
- **Narrative function**: CELEBRATES participation. This beat is genuinely awe-inspiring and should feel that way. From 19 crore voters in 1957 to 97 crore in 2024 -- a 5x expansion in 67 years. Turnout has hovered between 55-67% across decades, which means 60+ crore people voluntarily show up. That is not a statistic; it is a civilizational commitment. The state-level variation adds texture: Lakshadweep at 80%+, Bihar at 50%-something. But the dominant note is pride.
- **Key data**: Line chart -- national turnout 1957-2024. Peak year annotated. Event chips (Emergency, Mandal, first EVM, NOTA introduced). Choropleth -- state turnout 2024. Highest vs lowest state gap.
- **Emotional register**: Awe / pride
- **Transition to next**: 64 crore people voted. But who did they choose? India's political landscape has been rewritten three times in 67 years.

### Beat 2: How India's Political Map Changed (Party Landscape)
- **Narrative function**: ESTABLISHES context. This is the system's evolution, told as three eras. Congress dominance (1957-1984) -- a single party winning 75% of seats. Then fragmentation (1989-2009) -- no party crosses 240, coalitions govern, regional parties hold the balance. Then BJP's ascent (2014-2024) -- single-party dominance returns. 2024 is the turn: BJP drops from 303 to 240, INDIA alliance pushes them below majority for the first time in a decade. The stacked area chart makes the eras viscerally clear.
- **Key data**: Stacked area chart -- seats by party, 1957-2024. Three era callout cards. INC averaged 325 seats across Congress Era. No party crossed 240 during Coalition Era. BJP: 282 to 303 to 240.
- **Emotional register**: Curiosity / historical weight
- **Transition to next**: The eras show the SYSTEM shifting. But what happened in 2024 specifically? Zoom in.

### Beat 3: Lok Sabha 2024 -- The Verdict (Results)
- **Narrative function**: GROUNDS the story in the present. The Parliament hemicycle (543 dots, each a constituency) makes the composition tangible -- you see the BJP saffron, the INC blue, the regional scatter. NDA governs with 292 seats, relying on TDP and JD(U). The alliance comparison cards make the arithmetic immediate.
- **Key data**: Parliament hemicycle visualization. Alliance totals: NDA vs INDIA vs majority mark. Party bar chart (top 15 by seats). BJP 240, INC 99 (nearly doubled from 52).
- **Emotional register**: Engagement / informed tension
- **Transition to next**: **This is the pivot.** We've celebrated turnout, traced the system, examined the verdict. Now: who ARE these 543 people? What do their own sworn documents reveal?

### Beat 4: Money and Muscle in Parliament (Candidate Quality)
- **Narrative function**: INTERROGATES representation quality. This beat should hit hard. The data comes from candidates' own sworn affidavits -- not allegations, not media reports, but self-declarations filed under oath. X% have criminal cases. Y% face serious charges (murder, kidnapping, attempt to murder). The average MP is worth Z crore. The median is lower but still astronomical. The education breakdown is the one bright spot (most are graduates), but it doesn't offset the criminality numbers.
- **Key data**: Four stat cards (criminal cases, serious charges, avg assets, post-grads). Wealthiest MPs bar chart. Most criminal cases bar chart. Education stacked bar.
- **Emotional register**: Discomfort / quiet outrage
- **Transition to next**: It gets worse. Not only are many representatives wealthy and criminally charged -- half the population is systematically excluded from representation.

### Beat 5: The Gender Gap in Parliament (Women's Representation)
- **Narrative function**: CLOSES with the most structural failure. This beat should feel like a slow-building indictment. The line chart is nearly flat -- 67 years of elections, and women MPs went from ~5% to ~14%. Half the voters. 1 in 7 MPs. The 33% reservation law passed in 2023 after 27 years of attempts, but it is tied to delimitation and a new Census -- conditions that may not be met for a decade. The dashed line at 33% sits far above the actual curve, like a promise the system keeps deferring.
- **Key data**: Line chart -- women MPs (%), 1957-2024, with 33% target dashed line. Latest: X women in 543 seats (Y%). Global average callout: ~27%. India at 14%. Reservation Act callout card.
- **Emotional register**: Frustration / determination
- **Transition to next**: (Closing)

## Internal Consistency Rules

- **Denominator**: Always 543 Lok Sabha seats. Not Rajya Sabha, not state assemblies. One house, one denominator.
- **Time period**: Trend data covers 1957-2024 (all Lok Sabha elections). Candidate data is 2024 only.
- **Party naming**: Use abbreviations (BJP, INC, TMC) consistently. Full names in parentheses on first mention. No party colors in narrative text (colors live in the visualizations).
- **No political bias**: Present data. Do not editorialize about which party is better or worse. The criminal/wealth data is party-agnostic. Let citizens draw conclusions.
- **Source consistency**: ECI for turnout and results, ADR for candidate affidavits, TCPD for historical data.

## Closing

The closing should land the contradiction in the thesis:

"96.88 crore people are eligible to choose their representatives. They show up in extraordinary numbers. What they get in return -- a Parliament where 1 in 3 members faces criminal charges and 6 in 7 are men -- is the gap between India's democratic ambition and its democratic reality. The ballot box works. Everything around it needs work."
