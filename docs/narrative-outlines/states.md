# States -- Narrative Outline

## Headline Thesis

Being born in Goa versus being born in Bihar means growing up in two different countries that share the same flag.

## Arc Type

**Contradiction** -- India is one economy on paper, but 36 wildly unequal economies in practice. The federal structure is supposed to redistribute wealth and opportunity; the data shows it barely narrows the gap.

## Opening Hook

A child born in Goa today can expect per capita income 8-9x higher than a child born in Bihar. Same Constitution. Same central government. Same national anthem. The states page forces you to confront what "one nation" actually means when you zoom in.

## Beats (ordered)

### Beat 1: The Economic Engines (GSDP)
- **Narrative function**: Establishes the sheer scale of inequality. Maharashtra alone produces more than many nations. The bottom 10 states combined don't match it. This is not a gentle gradient -- it is a cliff.
- **Key data**: Maharashtra GSDP (approx 45 lakh crore) vs bottom states. Choropleth map showing the dark/light divide. Top 5 states produce more than half of India's output.
- **Emotional register**: Surprise / unease
- **Transition to next**: But maybe this is just about size? Big states produce more, small states less -- that would be fair. Except growth rates tell a different story.

### Beat 2: Who's Catching Up? (Growth Rates)
- **Narrative function**: ARGUES that convergence is not happening fast enough. If smaller/poorer states were growing faster, they'd be closing the gap. Some are (Tripura, Mizoram). But the big laggards (Bihar, UP, Jharkhand) -- home to 40 crore people -- grow at middling rates. The mosaic is hardening, not blurring.
- **Key data**: DotStrip with national median line. Highlight the top 5 and bottom 5. The critical question: are the bottom states above or below the median? If below, divergence is widening.
- **Emotional register**: Tension / concern
- **Transition to next**: Growth depends partly on resources. Where does the money come from? Some states can fund their own development. Others wait for Delhi to write the cheque.

### Beat 3: Who Pays Their Own Way? (Revenue Self-Sufficiency)
- **Narrative function**: ARGUES that fiscal independence determines a state's agency. States that raise their own revenue (Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka) can invest where they choose. States dependent on central transfers (Bihar, UP, Jharkhand) are structurally constrained -- they cannot even set their own priorities without the Centre's approval. This is the fairness question at the heart of Indian federalism.
- **Key data**: Stacked bar chart -- own tax revenue (emerald) vs central transfers (cyan). Self-sufficiency ratio annotation. The visual should make you feel the dependence: some bars are almost entirely cyan.
- **Emotional register**: Urgency / frustration
- **Transition to next**: Dependence is one thing. Recklessness is another. Some states that receive the most also borrow the most.

### Beat 4: The Weight of Debt (Fiscal Health)
- **Narrative function**: ARGUES that fiscal indiscipline creates a doom loop -- high debt leads to high interest payments, which crowd out schools and hospitals, which keeps the state poor, which keeps it borrowing. The FRBM 3% cap is supposed to prevent this. Several states ignore it. Green dots (surplus states) contrast with the stressed cluster.
- **Key data**: Scatter plot -- fiscal deficit vs debt-to-GSDP. FRBM 3% reference line. Punjab at 47% debt-to-GSDP. Annotation: "High-debt states spend more on interest payments than on schools or hospitals."
- **Emotional register**: Anger / alarm
- **Transition to next**: We've been measuring states by their output, their growth, their debt. But for a citizen, the only number that matters is: how much reaches me?

### Beat 5: The Real Standard of Living (Per Capita NSDP)
- **Narrative function**: LANDS the thesis. This is the punchline. When you divide by population, the hierarchy reshuffles. Delhi and Telangana leap ahead. Uttar Pradesh -- India's most populous state, a political powerhouse -- sits near the bottom. Per capita income is the closest proxy for "what does it feel like to live here?" The 8-9x gap between top and bottom is the single most damning number on this page.
- **Key data**: Choropleth + bar chart. National average line. The Goa-Bihar gap expressed as a ratio. "A worker in Goa produces in one month what a worker in Bihar produces in eight."
- **Emotional register**: Resolution / quiet anger
- **Transition to next**: (Closing)

## Internal Consistency Rules

- **Denominator**: All comparisons are state-vs-state. Never India-vs-world in this domain (that's Economy's job).
- **Time period**: Latest available year from RBI Handbook on Indian States. All sections use the same year.
- **Recurring framing**: "same country, different reality" -- this phrase or its variants appear in the hero, the opening bridge, and the closing. It is the spine.
- **Currency**: Always lakh crore for GSDP, crore for revenue, rupees for per capita. Never dollars.

## Closing

The closing does not tell you what to think. It tells you what to see:

"India's Constitution promises equality of opportunity. The data shows that the single biggest determinant of your economic life is which state you were born in. The mosaic is beautiful in theory. For the 22 crore people in Bihar, it is a cage."
