# Agriculture & Food Security -- Narrative Outline

## Thesis
Agriculture employs 42% of India's workforce but produces only 17% of GDP. This productivity gap keeps rural incomes low, makes food prices the primary inflation driver, and locks India into a water-intensive farming model that is depleting the resource it depends on most. The sector is simultaneously India's largest employer, its biggest water consumer, and its most stubborn structural challenge.

## Domains Used (and why)
- **Employment**: Sectoral employment shares, self-employment rate. The "how many people depend on agriculture?" side.
- **Economy**: GDP sectoral composition, CPI inflation (food-driven). The "how much does agriculture produce relative to its workforce?" and "how do food prices drive macro instability?" side.
- **Budget**: Food subsidies, fiscal deficit. The "how much does the government spend to keep food affordable?" side.
- **Environment**: Water stress, groundwater depletion, forest cover. The "is this farming model sustainable?" side.

## Argument Arc

### Beat 1: The Productivity Paradox
42% of workers produce 17% of GDP. Agricultural productivity per worker is a fraction of industry or services. The structural shift that pulled workers out of agriculture in China, South Korea, and others has not happened in India -- partly because manufacturing has not scaled to absorb them.
- Emotional register: Structural frustration.

### Beat 2: Food Prices and Inflation
Food accounts for ~46% of India's CPI basket. Vegetable and pulse price spikes can push headline inflation above 6%, triggering RBI rate hikes that cool the entire economy. Agriculture's weather-dependence makes food inflation a perennial macro risk.
- Emotional register: Connection between farm and kitchen table.

### Beat 3: Sustainability Pressures
Agriculture consumes ~80% of India's freshwater. Groundwater depletion, soil degradation, and climate variability (erratic monsoons) threaten long-term food security. Free electricity for farm pumps accelerated the Green Revolution but created an extraction economy with no brake. The transition to sustainable farming (drip irrigation, crop diversification, organic) is urgent but underinvested.
- Emotional register: Existential stakes for a sector that feeds 1.4 billion people.

## Why This Matters
Employment data says agriculture is the biggest employer. Economy data says it is the least productive sector. Budget data reveals the subsidy architecture that keeps food affordable. Environment data shows the water cost. Only together do they reveal the trap: India cannot stop supporting agriculture (too many livelihoods depend on it), cannot afford to continue as-is (water is running out), and cannot transition fast enough (manufacturing has not scaled). Agriculture is not a sector problem -- it is THE structural problem at the intersection of jobs, prices, water, and budgets.
