# India's Democratic Health -- Narrative Outline

## Thesis
India's elections are the world's largest logistical exercise, and turnout has risen steadily for seven decades. But democratic health is not just about showing up -- it is about who gets elected. Nearly half of MPs face criminal charges, average MP assets are 46 crore, and women hold 13.6% of seats. The gap between participation (high) and representation quality (low) is the central tension.

## Domains Used (and why)
- **Elections**: Turnout trend, criminal records of candidates, women's representation, MP assets. The core "who shows up and who gets elected?" data.
- **Census**: Literacy, education levels. The "is the electorate equipped to make informed choices?" baseline. Also demographic context for voter eligibility scale (96.88 crore electors).
- **Education**: Enrollment and learning outcomes. The long-term pipeline that shapes future electoral behaviour.

## Argument Arc

### Beat 1: The Participation Arc
Turnout has climbed from 48% (1957) to 67.4% (2019 peak), with 2024 at 65.8%. 96.88 crore electors were eligible. The act of voting is increasingly universal -- the franchise is working.
- Emotional register: Democratic pride.

### Beat 2: Quality of Representation
Nearly half of MPs elected in 2024 face criminal charges. Average declared assets: 46 crore. Women hold 13.6% of seats. The people who get elected are disproportionately wealthy, male, and legally entangled. The 33% reservation bill exists on paper but awaits delimitation with no timeline.
- Emotional register: Disillusionment, accountability demand.

## Why This Matters
Elections data alone celebrates turnout. Crime data alone condemns criminals. Census data alone measures literacy. Only together do they reveal the accountability gap: a well-educated electorate is growing, participation is strong, but the filtering mechanism that determines who becomes a candidate -- money, party tickets, dynastic networks -- produces a parliament that does not mirror the population. Democratic health requires both high turnout AND representative outcomes. India has achieved the first but not the second.
