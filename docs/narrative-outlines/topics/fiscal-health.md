# India's Fiscal Health -- Narrative Outline

## Thesis
The budget, the central bank, and GDP growth form a triangle: each constrains and enables the other two. No single domain reveals whether India's fiscal math is sustainable -- only the intersection does.

## Domains Used (and why)
- **Budget**: The spending and borrowing side -- fiscal deficit as % of GDP, total expenditure, revenue receipts. Provides the "how much does the government borrow?" answer.
- **Economy**: GDP growth rate and fiscal consolidation trend. Provides the "is the economy growing fast enough to outrun the debt?" answer.
- **RBI**: Repo rate and monetary policy stance. Provides the "what is the cost of borrowing, and is inflation under control?" answer.
- **States**: State-level fiscal health. Provides the "is the Centre's discipline matched by the states?" dimension.

## Argument Arc

### Beat 1: The Growth Engine
India grows at 6-7% -- the fastest major economy. This is the denominator in every fiscal ratio. If growth slows, deficits look worse even without spending more. The engine must keep running.
- Emotional register: Confidence tempered by fragility.

### Beat 2: Spending vs Borrowing
The fiscal deficit fell from 9.2% (COVID) to a target of 4.4% (2025-26). Consolidation is real, but the absolute deficit is still 15.69 lakh crore. For every rupee the government spends, about 28 paise is borrowed. The question: is this borrowing funding investment (good) or consumption (bad)?
- Emotional register: Tension between progress and scale.

### Beat 3: The Monetary Lever
The RBI's repo rate is the price of money. When inflation crossed 6%, the RBI hiked 250 bps in 10 months. Now it is cutting cautiously. Each 25 bps changes EMIs for millions. The RBI walks between two cliffs: inflation on one side, growth strangulation on the other.
- Emotional register: Precarious balance, high stakes for ordinary borrowers.

## Why This Matters
A citizen who reads only the Budget page sees spending. One who reads only the Economy page sees growth. One who reads only the RBI page sees interest rates. Only together do they see the triangle: the government borrows to spend, the RBI controls what borrowing costs, and growth determines whether the debt-to-GDP ratio improves or worsens. This is the central macro question of India's next decade.
