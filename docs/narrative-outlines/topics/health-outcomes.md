# Health Outcomes -- Narrative Outline

## Thesis
India's health system serves 1.4 billion people with fewer resources per capita than most peer nations. Progress is real -- infant mortality halved in 20 years, immunisation above 90% -- but the infrastructure deficit and the out-of-pocket burden mean that for most Indians, getting sick is a financial crisis before it is a medical one.

## Domains Used (and why)
- **Healthcare**: Hospital beds per 1,000, physician density, spending as % of GDP, out-of-pocket share, disease burden. The core "how good is the health system?" data.
- **Census**: IMR, MMR, life expectancy trends, NFHS-5 state-level health data. The population-level outcome measures that reveal whether the system is actually improving lives.
- **Budget**: Health allocation as share of total spending, Ayushman Bharat coverage. The "how much does the government prioritise health?" question.
- **States**: State-level variation in health infrastructure. The regional inequality dimension.

## Argument Arc

### Beat 1: Child Survival
IMR has dropped from 66 per 1,000 (2000) to about 25 today. DPT immunisation exceeds 90%. These are genuine achievements -- millions of children who would have died a generation ago are surviving. But state-level variation is wide: Kerala's IMR is single digits, while some states remain above 40.
- Emotional register: Progress, qualified by inequality.

### Beat 2: Infrastructure Gap
1.6 beds per 1,000 people (WHO recommends 3). 0.7 doctors per 1,000. Most beds are private. Rural areas have a fraction of urban infrastructure. This means millions of Indians travel hours for basic care, or go to unregulated private practitioners.
- Emotional register: Scarcity, systemic failure.

### Beat 3: Who Pays?
~45% of all health spending comes out of patients' pockets. Total health expenditure is ~3.3% of GDP (global average ~6%). A hospitalisation can wipe out a family's savings. Ayushman Bharat has expanded coverage but has not closed the financing gap. One medical emergency can push a non-poor family below the poverty line.
- Emotional register: Injustice, financial vulnerability.

## Why This Matters
Healthcare data alone shows beds and doctors. Census data alone shows mortality rates. Budget data alone shows allocation percentages. The intersection reveals the full circuit: India under-invests in health (Budget), which means insufficient infrastructure (Healthcare), which means poor families pay out of pocket (Healthcare + Budget), which means health shocks cause poverty (Census + Healthcare). The health system's failures are economic failures -- and vice versa.
