# Inflation & Cost of Living -- Narrative Outline

## Thesis
Inflation is the invisible tax that no one votes for. The intersection of price data, RBI policy, and budget spending reveals a triangle where each lever affects the other two -- and the citizen's wallet sits at the centre.

## Domains Used (and why)
- **Economy**: CPI headline and food inflation trends. The "what is happening to prices?" question.
- **RBI**: Repo rate decisions and monetary policy stance. The "what is the central bank doing about it?" question.
- **Budget**: Fiscal deficit and government spending. The "is government spending fuelling demand-pull inflation?" question.

## Argument Arc

### Beat 1: The Price Trajectory
CPI inflation averaged 6.7% in 2022-23, breaching the RBI's upper tolerance. Food inflation is the volatile driver -- vegetables and pulses can swing 10-20% in a single season. Food accounts for ~46% of India's CPI basket, making poor households disproportionately exposed.
- Emotional register: Recognition ("I feel this at the sabzi mandi").

### Beat 2: The RBI Response
When inflation crossed 6%, the RBI hiked the repo rate by 250 bps in 10 months. Each hike raises EMIs for millions of borrowers. Now, with inflation cooling, cautious cuts have begun. The RBI cannot control food supply shocks -- it can only suppress demand, which risks slowing growth.
- Emotional register: Understanding the tradeoff.

### Beat 3: The Fiscal Connection
Government spending can fuel demand-pull inflation. When the deficit widens, more money chases the same goods. The budget-inflation-RBI triangle is India's central macro balancing act. Fiscal consolidation (deficit falling from 9.2% to 4.4%) helps the RBI's job, but austerity has its own costs.
- Emotional register: Systemic clarity.

## Why This Matters
A citizen who checks tomato prices does not think about the repo rate. A citizen reading about the RBI does not think about the budget deficit. But these three forces are the same system. Inflation is where macroeconomics meets the kitchen table. This topic connects the abstract (monetary policy, fiscal deficit) to the tangible (your grocery bill, your EMI).
