# Regional Inequality -- Narrative Outline

## Thesis
A child born in Kerala and a child born in Bihar hold the same passport but will live in statistically different countries. Per capita income, education access, healthcare infrastructure, and life expectancy diverge so sharply across states that "India's average" is a fiction that obscures more than it reveals.

## Domains Used (and why)
- **States**: Per capita NSDP, GSDP growth, fiscal health. The economic backbone of the inequality argument -- the 7x gap between Delhi and Bihar.
- **Census**: Literacy rates, urbanization, demographic structure. The human development baseline that varies by state.
- **Education**: Secondary GER by state, enrollment disparities. The "equal opportunity" test -- does where you are born determine your education?
- **Healthcare**: Hospital beds per 1,000, health infrastructure by state. The "does your state determine whether you survive a medical emergency?" question.
- **Budget**: Finance Commission transfers, state allocations. The redistribution mechanism -- the Centre's attempt to level the playing field.

## Argument Arc

### Beat 1: The Income Divide
Per capita NSDP ranges from ~69,000 in Bihar to nearly 5 lakh in Delhi. Southern and western states cluster at the top; Bihar, UP, and Jharkhand cluster at the bottom. The gap has not narrowed in 20 years -- in fact, faster-growing states tend to be the already-rich ones.
- Emotional register: Stark inequality, discomfort.

### Beat 2: Education and Health Disparities
Kerala's secondary GER exceeds 100%. Bihar's is below 70%. Hospital beds per 1,000 vary from 0.3 in Bihar to over 2 in Kerala. These are not just numbers -- they determine whether a teenager in Patna has the same shot at college as a teenager in Kochi.
- Emotional register: Injustice at the geographic lottery of birth.

### Beat 3: Fiscal Transfers -- Does Redistribution Work?
The Finance Commission is supposed to redistribute -- poorer states receive more per capita from the Centre. But the mechanism is slow, formulaic, and does not track outcomes. Money flows, but capacity (administrative, institutional) to spend it well does not automatically follow.
- Emotional register: Systemic frustration, questioning whether the architecture is adequate.

## Why This Matters
National averages are comfortable. They let everyone claim progress. But India's regional inequality means that "India" is a statistical abstraction -- the lived reality differs by 7x depending on your pin code. No single domain captures this: you need income (States), human development (Census, Education, Healthcare), and the redistribution mechanism (Budget) to see the full picture. The uncomfortable question: is India one country or thirty different ones sharing a flag?
