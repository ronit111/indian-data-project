# Urban vs Rural Divide -- Narrative Outline

## Thesis
Two-thirds of India lives in villages, but cities command a disproportionate share of hospitals, schools, and economic output. The urban-rural divide is not narrowing -- it is shape-shifting. Urbanisation is accelerating, but urban slums can have worse outcomes than prosperous villages. The divide is about access, not just geography.

## Domains Used (and why)
- **Census**: Urbanisation rate, population growth, literacy by residence type. The foundational "who lives where?" data.
- **Employment**: LFPR and employment type by rural/urban. Urban workers are more likely to be salaried; rural workers are overwhelmingly self-employed or casual.
- **Healthcare**: Hospital beds per 1,000, health infrastructure. Urban areas have ~3x more beds per capita. The "does where you live determine whether you survive?" question.
- **Education**: School quality, enrollment by state (as a proxy for rural/urban mix). States with higher urbanisation tend to have better education outcomes.
- **Environment**: Air quality -- urban India breathes worse air, but rural India has less access to clean water.

## Argument Arc

### Beat 1: The Urbanisation Wave
Urban share has grown from 28% (2001) to ~35% today. By 2050, projections suggest half the population will be in cities. But this is not smooth diffusion -- it is mega-city concentration. Delhi, Mumbai, Bangalore absorb migration while smaller towns stagnate.
- Emotional register: Scale, speed, unevenness.

### Beat 2: The Access Gap
Urban areas have ~3x more hospital beds per capita, higher literacy, better school infrastructure. But rural India still houses 65% of the population -- small improvements there move national averages more than urban gains. The math of impact favours rural investment, but the politics of visibility favour urban spending.
- Emotional register: Structural unfairness.

## Why This Matters
The urban-rural divide is the meta-inequality beneath most other inequalities: gender gaps are wider in rural India, health outcomes are worse, employment is more precarious. No single domain captures this because the divide cuts across all of them. The insight: urbanisation is not solving the divide -- it is just moving the boundary line. Slum-dwellers in Mumbai may have worse sanitation than farmers in Punjab. "Urban" and "rural" are proxies for access, not guarantees of it.
