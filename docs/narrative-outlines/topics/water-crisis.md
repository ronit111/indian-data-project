# Water Crisis -- Narrative Outline

## Thesis
India has 18% of the world's population and 4% of its freshwater. The gap is worsening: groundwater depletion is irreversible on human timescales, agriculture consumes 80% of freshwater, and contaminated water kills an estimated 4 lakh people per year. This is not a future crisis -- it is a present one that most citizens cannot see.

## Domains Used (and why)
- **Environment**: Groundwater development stage by state, reservoir storage, water stress data. The physical evidence of depletion.
- **Census**: Population growth and density -- the demand side of the equation.
- **Healthcare**: Waterborne disease burden, hospital infrastructure, out-of-pocket health spending. The human cost of contaminated water.

## Argument Arc

### Beat 1: The Water Stress Map
Per capita water availability has dropped from 5,177 cubic metres in 1951 to below 1,500 today. India is classified as "water-stressed." By 2030, demand is projected to outstrip supply in many river basins. The map of groundwater extraction shows Punjab, Haryana, and Rajasthan in deep red -- extracting more than nature replenishes.
- Emotional register: Scale of the problem made geographic.

### Beat 2: Groundwater Depletion
India extracts more groundwater than the US and China combined. Punjab is at 165% extraction-to-recharge -- mining fossil water that will not return in our lifetimes. Free electricity for farm pumps, rice-wheat monoculture, and the Green Revolution's legacy have created an extraction economy with no off switch.
- Emotional register: Irreversibility, dread.

### Beat 3: Water and Health
Contaminated water drives waterborne diseases that kill an estimated 4 lakh Indians annually. Access to clean drinking water has improved (Jal Jeevan Mission), but water quality and sanitation in rural areas remain challenges. Out-of-pocket health spending means water-related illness pushes families into poverty.
- Emotional register: Human cost of a resource problem.

## Why This Matters
Water is the thread that connects agriculture, health, urbanisation, and climate. No single domain can tell the water story: you need Environment (how much water exists and where), Census (how many people depend on it), and Healthcare (what happens when it is contaminated or absent). The insight at the intersection: India's water crisis is not a drought problem -- it is a governance, agriculture, and infrastructure problem happening underground where no camera can capture it.
