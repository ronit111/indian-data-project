# Youth & Jobs -- Narrative Outline

## Thesis
India's demographic dividend is a ticking clock, not a guaranteed prize. 65% of the population is under 35, but youth unemployment is 2-3x the national average, and most new employment is self-employment in agriculture. The dividend becomes a burden if the jobs do not materialise.

## Domains Used (and why)
- **Employment**: Youth unemployment rate, LFPR, sectoral employment shares. The core "are there enough jobs?" data.
- **Education**: Enrollment scale, secondary GER, spending as % of GDP. The "are we preparing young people?" pipeline.
- **Census**: Age structure, dependency ratio, demographic window. The "how long do we have?" clock.
- **Economy**: GDP growth, sectoral GDP shares. The "is growth creating the right kind of jobs?" check.

## Argument Arc

### Beat 1: The Youth Challenge
Youth (15-29) unemployment is consistently 2-3x the national average. A painful paradox: the more educated you are, the higher your unemployment rate. A graduate is more likely to be unemployed than someone who never finished school -- because graduates hold out for formal-sector jobs that do not exist in sufficient numbers.
- Emotional register: Paradox, frustration.

### Beat 2: Who's Working?
Overall LFPR at ~46.5% masks a massive gender gap (male ~78%, female ~28.8%). Many who are counted as "employed" are in agriculture or unpaid family work. The headline employment number flatters a reality where quality of work is the real crisis.
- Emotional register: Scepticism toward headline numbers.

### Beat 3: Quality of Work
Over half the workforce is self-employed, mostly in agriculture. Manufacturing's share has stagnated around 12% for decades. Services are growing but concentrated in urban areas. The "good jobs" challenge is structural -- India needs a manufacturing surge that has not arrived.
- Emotional register: Urgency, structural concern.

## Why This Matters
India's median age is 28. China's is 39. This demographic advantage lasts roughly until 2050, then the window closes as the population ages. Every year without enough productive jobs is a year of the dividend wasted. No single domain captures this urgency -- only the intersection of employment data, education pipeline capacity, and demographic structure reveals how much time India has and how poorly it is using it.
